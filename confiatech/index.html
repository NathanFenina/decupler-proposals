Reading it correctlyThis isn't a decline — it's a launch curve. Google indexed essentially nothing for 8 of the last 12 months, then started picking up pages from December 2025 (1 keyword), accelerating to 17 by August 2026. confiatech.com is a genuinely young domain in Google's eyes, whatever its actual delivery history. The question isn't "why did we lose visibility" — it's "how do we feed this curve on purpose instead of by accident."

Technical — schema.org (verified on the live site)

Genuinely thorough for a site this size: Organization, ProfessionalService, WebSite, WebPage, two ItemList blocks (12 services), two FAQPage blocks, BlogPosting on every article. Mobile PageSpeed 91/100 · good. This is not the gap.

Technical — two bugs found while checking the above

1) /blog/5-ways-to-upgrade-your-home-decor-with-google-search is listed in your own BlogPosting schema (dated Aug 25) but returns the homepage shell, not the post, when fetched without JavaScript — a soft-404 that both Google and any AI crawler that doesn't render JS will see as duplicate homepage content. 2) Your FAQPage schema claims a "5-star Clutch rating" and 32 reviews — verified: no link to a Clutch profile exists on the homepage, blog, or case-studies page. An unverifiable trust claim is worse than no claim.

What the AI is actually basing these citations on

Reading all 30 raw responses, the same three signals justify almost every citation — and none of them are about code quality.

Scale & listing status

"One of the largest," "publicly listed"

"Systems Limited — one of Pakistan's largest IT companies... publicly listed" · "NetSol Technologies — NASDAQ-listed... global presence." The model repeats a reputation signal it has read elsewhere, not something it evaluated on the company's own site.

Named specialization + proof

A category and a recognizable client

"Arbisoft — known for AI/ML and data science... works with international clients including Coursera." This is exactly ConfiaTech's own positioning (AI-augmented delivery) — but a competitor gets credit for it, backed by one citable client name.

Category framing

A sector the model can name

"NetSol Technologies... particularly in the finance and leasing sectors." Companies the model can slot into a recognizable category get named; companies it can't categorize don't.

What this means for ConfiaTechNone of the 30 responses mention a claim ConfiaTech could make today (AI-augmented delivery, 8-plus-year engineers, 200+ platforms shipped, 98% retention). The model doesn't know these facts — because they exist only on confiatech.com itself, in a form nothing outside the site repeats back.

The one that connects directly to chapter 05Clutch.co is named in 4 of the 30 responses as where to check a software company's credentials — and it's also the exact platform ConfiaTech's own schema.org claims a "5-star rating, 32 reviews" on, with zero link a reader or crawler could follow to confirm it (see Opportunities). This is the single highest-leverage fix in this audit: the AI already tells buyers where to look; right now, what they'd find there is unverifiable.
05Opportunities

The highest-leverage moves

Verified against the live site before being written here — not generic assumptions.

Fix now

Fix the soft-404 blog post

One published post (dated Aug 25, live in your own schema) returns the homepage shell instead of its content when rendered without JavaScript — verified. A quick, concrete technical fix, not a content rewrite.

Content

Point the blog at buyer intent

9 of the last 11 posts are AI-research recaps (Granite 4.2, KVBoost benchmarks) — interesting, but not about staff augmentation, MVP delivery, or RAG for enterprises, the services you actually sell. Zero posts target a buyer-intent keyword — which is exactly why zero rank for one.

Trust · highest leverage

Back the Clutch claim with a link

Your own FAQPage schema states "5-star Clutch rating, 32 reviews" — verified: no outbound link to a Clutch profile exists anywhere on the site. Clutch.co is also the platform the AI itself names most often as where to check a vendor (chapter 04) — right now, what a buyer or a crawler would find there is unverifiable.

Verified — not a gapYour schema.org implementation (Organization, ProfessionalService, 2× FAQPage, 2× ItemList, BlogPosting on every article) is more thorough than most agencies twice your size — checked directly in the page source, not assumed. This is not where the work is needed. The gap is upstream: nothing points search engines or AI models at this well-structured foundation yet.
0612-Month Roadmap

Three workstreams, one system

Fix what's broken, align content to the business, then earn the citations — and measure again.

Q1 · Months 1-3 — Fix & anchor

Close the verified gaps

  • Fix the soft-404 blog post and audit the rest of the blog for the same rendering issue
  • Link the Clutch claim to a real profile, or remove the claim
  • 1st page built to actually target a buyer-intent keyword (e.g. "AI-augmented software development company")
Q2 · Months 4-6 — Content

Align the blog to the business

  • 3-5 posts on staff augmentation, MVP delivery, RAG for enterprises — the actual services
  • Case studies (already 20 real ones) linked into a structured, crawlable portfolio
  • 2nd GEO measurement on the same 10-prompt panel
Q3 · Months 7-9 — Authority

Get named by the AI

  • Third-party proof (Clutch, G2, comparison articles) — the exact signal the model repeats for competitors
  • Comparison content against the 4 named competitors (chapter 02)
  • E-E-A-T reinforcement: named engineers, verifiable claims, sources the AI can cite
Q4 · Months 10-12 — Review

Measure & re-plan

  • Full SEO + GEO re-audit vs. the August 2026 baseline
  • Recovered traffic and citations vs. targets agreed together
  • Next year's priorities
